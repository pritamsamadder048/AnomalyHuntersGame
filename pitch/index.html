02  What it is

The short version

A real investigation, on a real clock.

You take a contract from the Bureau, travel to a haunted site, and work it the way an investigator actually would. Arrive at 6 AM in daylight. Survey the property. Place and calibrate your equipment while you can still see. Then sit the night out from a van or tent outside the building as something begins to answer your instruments. After 9 PM, it can kill you.

03  The problem

Why this genre burns out

Every ghost hunting game is the same puzzle.

Phasmophobia, Demonologist, Ghost Watchers — read three instruments, match the numbers to a wiki table, type the ghost’s name. It is memorisable. And once it is memorised, the game is over; what remains is friends messing about in a dark room.

The genre today

“EMF 5 plus freezing plus writing — that’s a Demon. Go.”

  • Arrive at night, dump all equipment on the floor in ninety seconds
  • The answer is a species name from a fixed table
  • The wiki finishes the game for the player in week one
  • Content updates only add rows to the same table

Anomaly Hunters

“The wage book says he was eleven months unpaid. The tank steps are worn. Photograph the register.”

  • A full daylight shift: survey, place, calibrate, read the house
  • The answer is a history — who, why, what binds, what releases
  • Nothing to memorise, because the story changes every case
  • Content updates are new stories, and stories don’t spoil the old ones

04  The core idea

The anti-wiki property

You investigate a case, not a species.

Instruments tell you how strong a presence is, that something just happened, and which object or room it cares about. They never name it. A photograph of a buried pot is a lead — a photograph of that pot taken while the meter spiked is proof the pot matters.

SlotThe questionWorked example — Case 001, the Rajbari, 1911
Victim Who is it? Nabin Chandra Das, household tutor, d. 1911
Cause Why is it still here? Drowned in the estate tank; body recovered, rites never performed
Binding What holds it here? His spectacles, locked in the office chest ever since
Release What ends it? Immersion at water, his name spoken, the spectacles released

Why this is the commercial argument. A player can memorise an EMF table in an afternoon. They cannot memorise a history that is different every case. The slots are structured picks, not free text — so the game can verify a filed report with no AI and no language model anywhere in the product.

05  Core loop

One contract, start to finish

Daylight is half the game.

HQ

Bureau

Walkable hub. Assemble the team, recruit a hunter at the personnel desk, take a case from a client NPC, buy gear and pick a base.

06:00 – 17:00

The day shift

Survey, place and calibrate equipment in full light. Find documents, objects and physical traces that only exist by day.

17:00 – 21:00

First activity

It starts answering. Evidence gathering at real but survivable risk. Ward your base room now, or don’t.

21:00 →

The haunting

It hunts. It phases through walls but not iron. It can wear a teammate’s face. Charms and mantras buy you one mistake.

Extract

Bank it

Return to base and extract. The only way to keep money, XP and everything in your pack.

Real stakes, unlike the genre standard. Death removes that character for good — their level, their gear and everything in the van go with them, and only the account-wide locker survives. There is no quit-to-menu-and-keep-everything.

06  Differentiators

Four things nobody else has

Defensible, not cosmetic.

01

The real workflow

Designed by a working paranormal investigator. Daylight setup, baseline calibration, monitoring from outside the building. Bad calibration means you miss real activity later. No competitor models the day shift at all.

02

A case, not a name

Victim, cause, binding, release — recovered from documents you photograph, objects you find and rites you recognise. The story differs every case even when the species does not.

03

Consequence

A roster of named specialists you recruit and can permanently lose, extraction-only payouts, gear that genuinely goes. The tension of an extraction shooter applied to a genre that has never had any.

04

A new mythology

South Asian folklore, researched rather than borrowed. Iron, salt lines, sigils and mantras as working mechanics. An entire continent of source material the genre has never touched.

07  Characters & crew

Who you send in

You don’t play a slot. You play someone who can die.

A player keeps up to five hunters. Each one is recruited with earned money at the Bureau’s personnel desk, belongs to a class, and carries their own stats, level and service record. Your teammates can see what class you brought — and nothing else about your roster. Deciding who walks into the house is the first real decision of a contract.

01

Specialists, not skins

Investigator, Technician, Occultist, Psychic, Medium, Tantrik — each with its own psychic power, strength and stamina, its own abilities and its own starting kit. A team that brought no Medium works the case differently.

02

Recruited, never respawned

A death removes that person from the roster and takes their gear and the van with them. The account locker survives, so a wipe costs a run rather than a save file — harsh enough to matter, never harsh enough to quit over.

03

Assembled at HQ

Set the team size, invite friends, then decide whether to open what’s left to strangers — wait for a full crew, or take a short window and drive out with whoever turned up. Positional team voice throughout.

And it is a content seam. A class is a data asset, so a new one ships like a case pack does. Owning a class and recruiting one are deliberately separate steps: even a class from a paid pack still costs earned in-game money every time you put someone in a slot.

08  Setting & content

Edition 1: India

Hauntings as grief, not monsters.

A Bengali rajbari, a Tamil temple complex, a Goan colonial mansion. Bhoota, Pret, Pishacha. Every case is written so the player finishes it feeling the haunting was the reasonable response of someone who was wronged — and cover-ups happen because a family was frightened, poor or ashamed, never because anyone was evil.

Editions

A four-region roadmap

Edition 1 ships India. Editions 2–4 expand across Southeast, East and South Asia — each a paid expansion with its own entities, locations and folklore rules.

Story packs

Content that ships cheap

A case is a data asset plus its props and audio, cooked into its own pack. The core game never needs to know a pack exists — which makes authored stories a low-cost, high-margin DLC unit.

Generation

Infinite plus authored

A generator builds coherent cases from fragment pools for endless play; hand-written cases carry the moments a generator can’t. Both produce the same runtime structure.

09  Market

Who buys this

A proven genre with an unserved half.

Co-op ghost hunting is one of the most commercially durable indie categories of the last five years. What it has never served is the player who wants the investigation rather than the screaming — the sim audience, the folklore audience, and the very large lapsed audience who finished the wiki and left.

Core

Co-op horror groups

Friend groups of 2–4 who want real division of labour — someone on the monitors, someone in the house — not four people holding the same torch.

Adjacent

Simulation & detective players

The audience for slow, procedural, competence-driven games. Calibration, documentation and deduction are the appeal, not a friction to skip.

Underserved

South Asia & the diaspora

A rapidly growing PC market that has almost never seen its own folklore treated seriously in a game. This is a marketing asset as much as a design one.

The pillar we will not trade away In-game currency is earned only and can never be bought. Every mechanical item — including the tent that carries more equipment — is bought with money the player earned on a contract. A purchased class is no exception: buying it lets you recruit that specialist, and each recruit still costs earned money. It adds a character, never a shortcut.

Pricing is the current plan for a Steam-first release with regional pricing, and is not final until the Early Access date is announced.

11  Where it stands

Solo developer, AI-assisted, Unreal Engine 5.8

Not a pitch deck. A running build.

  • Live

    The contract loop closes, end to end. A case is offered by an HQ client NPC, accepted, chosen at the van, run on site against a day limit, extracted for money and XP, settled back into a board where the clients you ignored have lost patience, and reported on a post-contract results screen.

  • Live

    Economy and persistence reconcile across a full round trip — carried gear, locker, shared van contents and payouts all balance, plus a courier resupply that delivers from your locker to the van mid-contract for a fee by weight.

  • Live

    Characters and permadeath. Five-slot roster, six classes, server-validated recruiting at the personnel desk, per-character levels and stats, and a death that settles at extraction and takes the character with it — driven through the real UI in engine.

  • Live

    Multiplayer between two real clients on separate accounts: lobby, start, travel into a shared session. Team size, friend invites, matchmaking with a fill window and positional voice are built and exercised; hosting no longer reloads the hub.

  • Live

    Documentation pays. The camera resolves a subject in frame, banks it at extraction, and pays a one-off discovery bounty the first time anyone on the account documents that subject — verified persisting across separate sessions.

  • Built

    The case black box. Victim, cause, binding and release behind a prerequisite graph of clues, produced identically by a fragment generator or a hand-written story, and machine-checked for solvability at save and at cook — an unfinishable case cannot ship. Case 001 is authored and validates.

  • Built

    Goal-driven entity AI. A StateTree ghost with a folklore-grounded day/night power curve, partial manifestation, binding to a cursed relic that moves the haunting when you move it, and haunt events on real physical props.

  • Built

    Equipment, HUD and the world layer. Handheld tools with real sensing, tool readouts, throwable and interactable props, doors, room lights and a mains breaker that darkens the whole house, tiered operations terminals, and the Bureau HQ hub.

  • Next

    Finishing the investigation layer — the case file screen, the lore handbook that carries progression, and entity disposition, so that not every haunting is hostile and player behaviour moves it.

  • Next

    Content to launch — two further launch entities, class abilities and character art, final location art, and polish. Roadmap targets 16–18 months to full launch, with Early Access ahead of it.
